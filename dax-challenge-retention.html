Who came back?

Marketing wants one retention number they can trust. In any month, how many customers bought again after buying the month before? This one takes real filter-context thinking.

The brief

  • A customer is retained in a month if they ordered in that month and in the month before.
  • Each customer counts once, however many orders they placed.
  • It must work in a visual by month, and respond to the City filter. January looks back to December of the previous year.
  • Write one measure. Any approach is welcome: you are graded on the numbers it produces, not on how it looks. It is also tested on a second dataset you can’t see.

Stuck? Two nudges.

Hint 1: how to think about it

Think in tables, not numbers. You need the list of customers who bought in the current month, and the list of customers who bought in the previous month. Then the question is which customers are on both lists.

Hint 2: the tools

One function compares two tables by their rows; a time-intelligence function can shift the dates in the filter context by a month. Variables (VAR) keep the two lists readable. There is more than one way to get there.
