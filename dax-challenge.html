What does an average order look like?

A quick one to start. The sales team wants the average value of an order, for any month or city they pick. Write the measure, see it match the target, and unlock the next level.

The brief

  • Average order value is total sales ÷ number of orders.
  • You get a ready-made measure, [Total Sales]. Every row of the Sales table is one order.
  • It must respond to the month and City filters, and never show an error.
  • Write one measure. Any approach is welcome: you are graded on the numbers it produces, not on how it looks.

The data is fictional. Download it as CSV to try the measure in your own Power BI Desktop.

Stuck? Two nudges.

Hint 1: how to think about it

You already have the numerator: [Total Sales]. The missing piece is how many orders there were. Orders are the rows of the Sales table, and each has its own OrderID.

Hint 2: the tools

Count the orders with COUNTROWS or DISTINCTCOUNT, and divide with DIVIDE so an empty month never causes an error. There is more than one way to get there.

The full walkthrough, with several approaches, will be shared on LinkedIn once the challenge closes.
